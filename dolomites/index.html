04 · Table

Food & delicacies

What to actually order, and where to sit down and order it.

Belluno's Dolomites sit at a crossroads of Venetian and Tyrolean cooking — expect butter and poppy seed as often as tomato.

The signature dish

Casunziei ampezzani

Half-moon ravioli filled with sweet beetroot (sometimes pumpkin or spinach), dressed simply in melted butter, poppy seeds and smoked ricotta. Born in Cortina d'Ampezzo — order it there first, then compare versions elsewhere in the valley.

Bread dumplings

Canederli

Large bread dumplings studded with speck, cheese or spinach, served either swimming in broth or simply sauced with melted butter — a legacy of the area's Tyrolean neighbours.

Pan-fried fresh cheese

Schiz in padella

A fresh, milky cow's-cheese from the Belluno malghe (mountain dairies), pan-fried in butter until it just holds together — simple, rich, and usually served with polenta.

Cheesy polenta

Toc' in braide

Soft polenta worked with melted local cheese and butter until almost molten — proper mountain comfort food, often the first thing offered at a rifugio.

Roe deer & venison

Capriolo & cervo

Slow-braised game, usually in a rich red-wine sauce with juniper, ladled over polenta — an autumn-leaning dish that mountain restaurants serve year-round.

Try it at Al Capriolo, Vodo di Cadore ↗
Cured ham & hearty soups

Speck & mountain barley soup

Lightly smoked, air-cured ham as a starter, and thick barley-and-bean soups (orzo e fagioli) as the valley's answer to a cold day on the trail.

The Valle dei Gelatieri

Gelato artigianale

Val di Zoldo and the wider Cadore are known as the "Valley of the Gelato Makers" — from the late 1800s, hundreds of local artisans emigrated across Europe carrying the craft with them. A gelato stop in Forno di Zoldo or Zoldo Alto is tasting the source, not a tourist add-on.

To finish

Grappa

A short, sharp spirit to close a mountain dinner — most rifugi and osterie pour a local, small-producer bottle rather than a mass-market one if you ask.

To toast

Prosecco Superiore

On a lowland day out in the Conegliano-Valdobbiadene hills, a glass of the UNESCO-listed Prosecco Superiore DOCG at source — a different, gentler style to the everyday Prosecco DOC.

06 · Culture

Art, churches & architecture

Renaissance piazzas, a Romanesque sanctuary, and two very different bridges — one Venetian and monumental, one Tyrolean-style and slung across a gorge.

Drive ~10 min · Stay 1.5–2 hrs

Piazza del Duomo, Belluno

Belluno's monumental square in one stop: the Venetian Gothic-Renaissance Palazzo dei Rettori (1409–1536), the Cathedral with its Baroque bell tower designed by Filippo Juvarra, and the Palazzo Fulcis museum with works by Bartolomeo Montagna, Tintoretto and Andrea Brustolon. The nearby Church of Santo Stefano holds frescoes attributed to Jacopo da Montagnana.

Directions from base ↗ View on Google Maps ↗
Drive ~30 min · Stay 45 min

Chiesa di Santa Maria Immacolata, Longarone

A striking modern church by Florentine architect Giovanni Michelucci, built 1975–1983 on the site of the parish church destroyed by the 1963 Vajont wave. Two spiralling concrete amphitheatres, one open to the sky overlooking the dam — a memorial as much as a place of worship, and one of the 20th century's more unusual sacred buildings.

Quiet find
Directions from base ↗ View on Google Maps ↗
Drive ~35 min · Stay 1 hr

Santuario dei Santi Vittore e Corona, Feltre

The last intact Romanesque building in the province, perched on a rock spur above Feltre since 1096. Byzantine-influenced frescoes from the Giotto school line the interior, and a beautiful 15th-century cloister sits alongside — reached by a dramatic 19th-century staircase.

Quiet find
Directions from base ↗ View on Google Maps ↗
Drive ~10 min · Stay 20–30 min

Ponte della Vittoria, Belluno

A single 65-metre concrete arch thrown across the Piave in 1923–1926 by Venetian engineer Eugenio Miozzi, deliberately built without piers so floodwater couldn't erode it. Its decorative panels, by local architect Riccardo Alfarè, carry verses from the WWI "Song of the Piave" — a graceful, symbolic piece of engineering.

Directions from base ↗ View on Google Maps ↗
Drive ~50 min · Stay 1.5–2 hrs

Ponte tibetano della Val Montina, Perarolo di Cadore

A 100m+ steel suspension footbridge opened in 2018, strung roughly 90m above the Valmontina torrent to open up a wild, near-inaccessible valley. A short, family-manageable walk from the car park — not for anyone with a fear of heights, but a genuinely impressive piece of engineering in a dramatic setting.

07 · Day trip

Venice for the day

A proper change of scene — canals and crowds instead of passes and pines. It's a full day out, so start early and pick a handful of must-dos rather than trying to see everything.

Good to know for your dates

  • No access fee needed. Venice's day-tripper "Contributo di Accesso" only applies on 60 selected dates between 3 April and 26 July 2026 — none of which fall in your week, so nothing to book or register for a visit in August.
  • Bridges everywhere. Venice has hundreds of stepped bridges and no ramps on most of them — a baby carrier will get you around far more easily than a pushchair.
  • It's a long day. Door to door is close to 4 hours of travel round trip, so an early start pays off.
Getting there
~1 h 45–2 h · recommended

Direct train

Regional trains run straight from Belluno to Venezia Santa Lucia — around 25 a day, no changes, and you step off right onto the Grand Canal. No need to book far ahead, but grab tickets a few days out in August. Much easier than driving with a baby in tow.

10 · Before you go

Book & plan ahead

1–8 August sits just before Ferragosto (15 Aug) — busy, but not the absolute peak.

Things to book

  • Lago di Braies parking / shuttle — mandatory online slot for 09:00–16:00, 1 Jul–15 Sep 2026, if driving.
  • Tre Cime toll-road parking at Rifugio Auronzo — fills up and is best pre-booked in August.
  • Cortina dinners — SanBrite, Baita Fraina and La Tavernetta all get busy; book a few days ahead.
  • Lagazuoi & Marmolada cable cars — check current timed-entry rules in peak season.
  • Carbon road bike hire — confirm frame size and availability with the shop before travelling.
  • Any rifugio overnight stay — if extending a hike onto Alta Via 1, book weeks ahead.

Get there before it's busy

  • Lago di Braies — before 9:00am, when the car access barrier comes down.
  • Tre Cime / Rifugio Auronzo car park — by 8:00am.
  • Lago di Sorapis trailhead (Passo Tre Croci) — by 8:00am, parking is limited.
  • Cinque Torri chairlift — before 10:00am to avoid the midday queue.
  • Lago di Santa Croce — before 11:00am, for calm water and easy parking.
General notes

Distances and drive times are approximate from Pittanzella and will run longer in August traffic on the passes. Cable car hours, toll roads and Braies booking rules should be reconfirmed close to travel, as they shift seasonally.
